What entry-level data engineer jobs ask for, and where senior jobs differ

November 5, 2026 · 6 min read

The Jobs Index holds about 576,000 open job postings. Some of them are data engineering roles, and their titles tell me the level: "Junior Data Engineer", "Data Engineer I", "Associate Data Engineer", "Data Engineering Intern". Others say "Senior", "Lead", "Staff" or "Principal".

So I asked a simple question: what does an entry-level data engineering posting ask for, and how is that different from a senior one?

The answer is useful from both sides of the hiring table. Hiring managers can see what a normal junior posting looks like. Job seekers can see what to learn first and what can wait.

The data

  • Snapshot: the export from November 4, 2026, with 576,208 open postings.
  • Data engineering: postings whose title says data engineer, analytics engineer, data platform engineer, big data engineer, ETL engineer, DataOps, "engenheiro de dados" or "ingeniero de datos".
  • Level, from the title only: - Entry: junior, jr, intern, trainee, estagiário, graduate, entry, associate, new grad, or "Engineer I". - Senior+: senior, sr, III, IV, lead, staff, principal. - Titles like "Associate Director", manager, director and head are left out.
  • Duplicates: the same title at the same company counts once.

That leaves 67 entry-level postings and 875 senior+ postings. Another 924 data engineering postings have no level in the title and are not used here.

A skill counts when the full job description names it anywhere. That makes these numbers "mentioned", not "required": a tool listed under "nice to have" counts the same as one under "must have".

What the table says

The foundation doesn't change with level. SQL and Python show up in about 80% of postings at every level. AWS and Databricks are almost the same too. A junior and a senior data engineer are expected to speak the same base language.

The gap is production work. The skills that grow the most with seniority are the ones you need to run data systems, not just write queries:

  • Streaming: 10% → 41%
  • Data modeling: 31% → 54%
  • Airflow: 15% → 37%
  • dbt: 15% → 35%
  • CI/CD: 33% → 52%
  • Kafka: 6% → 25%

Some skills show up more in entry-level posts. Git (46% vs 34%), BI tools (30% vs 24%) and Docker (19% vs 12%) are mentioned more often in entry postings. My guess is that junior postings spell out basics that senior postings take for granted, and that many entry roles sit closer to analytics.

GenAI is the same at both levels. About 28% of postings mention LLMs or generative AI, junior or senior. It's not a senior-only topic anymore.

Entry postings are not short. The median entry posting names 6 of the tools I track, against 9 for senior+. 36% of entry postings name 8 or more, against 63% of senior+.

For hiring managers

If you're writing a junior data engineering posting, here is what most of them look like: SQL, Python, one cloud, Git, some data quality. That's the market's baseline for the level.

Kafka (6% of entry postings), streaming (10%) and Kubernetes (12%) are rare in junior posts. If yours asks for them as requirements, you are competing for senior candidates with a junior offer, and the juniors who could grow into the role may not apply.

If the role really needs them, say what the person will learn on the job instead of listing the tools as must-haves.

For job seekers

SQL and Python get you through the door. Add one cloud (AWS shows up most often) and Git.

Then learn what separates the levels: orchestration (Airflow, dbt), CI/CD for data, and data modeling. These have the biggest gaps, and you can practice all of them in a personal project.

Streaming and Kafka can wait until you're past your first role.

Checking my own numbers

The first version of these numbers was wrong in a few places. I found that by reading matches by hand.

I read the matched text in 40 postings, 20 entry and 20 senior+, for the skills with the biggest gaps. Airflow, dbt, Kafka and CI/CD matches were all real. Three things needed fixing:

  • Streaming. My first pattern had no word boundary at the start, so it also matched "downstream", "upstream" and "mainstream". It also counted any "real-time", including a company tagline. Now a posting counts if it says stream/streaming or names a streaming tool (Kafka, Kinesis, Flink, Pub/Sub, Event Hubs, change data capture). That took entry from 18% to 10% and senior+ from 49% to 41%. The gap stayed at 31 points.
  • Data modeling. "Build pipelines and data models" is a duty, not a modeling skill. Now it counts only "data modeling", dimensional modeling, star schema, Kimball, data vault or normalization. Entry went from 41% to 31%, senior+ from 59% to 54%.
  • Misses. CI/CD missed postings that only name GitHub Actions, Jenkins or Azure DevOps. GenAI missed "large language model" written out, "prompt engineering" and "agentic". Adding them moved CI/CD up 2–3 points and GenAI from 25% / 22% to 28% / 29%. The second finding changed with it: GenAI is not more common in junior posts, just equal.

Removing postings with the same title at the same company also took the groups from 73 to 67 entry and from 1,032 to 875 senior+.

Why there is no trend

I wanted to show how entry-level requirements are changing. The data doesn't support it yet:

  • The groups are tiny. By the week each posting was first seen, there are 4, 6, 28 and 35 entry postings (up to Oct 12, Oct 13–19, Oct 20–26, Oct 27–Nov 4).
  • "First seen" isn't "posted". It moves forward every time I add a new set of career boards, so a newer week partly means newer sources.
  • Only open postings have descriptions. The older weeks only contain postings that are still open, and closed postings in the history keep their titles but not their descriptions.

I'll come back to this when there are a few months of history.

Caveats

  • 67 entry postings is a small group. One or two companies with many similar postings can move a share by several points.
  • Big-company bias. Most entry postings come from Workday and Greenhouse boards, so large employers are over-represented.
  • Level from title only. Many postings never say their level, and "Associate" means different things at different companies.
  • Keyword matching. A mention is not a requirement, and a pattern can still miss a phrasing or catch a wrong one. The spot check reduced this but didn't remove it.
  • One snapshot. These are open postings on November 4, 2026.
